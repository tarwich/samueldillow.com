AI is bringing a lot of potential to the software landscape and we could do more to take advantage of it. There are some significant fears we should address, not ignore. However, in light of those concerns we shouldn't shrink back from growth. Instead, we should use those hurdles to learn and grow through the mistakes. This doesn't mean letting AI take the reins. AI first means we lead with AI and step in where appropriate, learning from problems and growing along the way.

We've all heard stories of the amazing work AI can do, but most of our dev team is leery to take full advantage of it. They have good reason: AI has fumbled really bad, and continues to do so on this project. Most of the time when AI writes a feature it either poorly understands the codebase, overengineers something that becomes brittle, or does an excellent job but doesn't follow our rules. 

When you consider the faceplants AI has done for us and for other companies, you have to ask yourself: do I really want AI vibe coding an aviation management platform in a tightly regulated space? Do I really want to put the lives of my customers in the hands of an AI that proves it's not only capable of making mistakes, but guaranteed to do so again? My controversial answer is YES we do.

I'm not saying we switch to vibe coding and hope for the best. I'm recommending an AI-first approach where we surface problems as a team, and solve them as a team. Any time an issue or feature comes into the funnel, it goes to AI first. Then the team looks at what went wrong and develops mitigations for that problem. Then a team member can take over and finish the task or they can continue to iterate with AI.

Here's the really hot take: AI might make errors, but so do humans. It's like hiring a team of employess: they're not always going to do the job as good as you would, but you must learn to coach them toward mututal success. I constantly get annoyed when I see AI products with errors that a normal person would've caught and fixed, but that's going to be the new normal. The important thing is not to be error free, but to be safe from costly errors.

Once we get this established we'll be able to better support the growing number of team members using AI. Drew made a feature with AI that could've made it into the product (or A product if not Airpeggio). Parker is using AI to help with social media and we might be able to share how we've built guardrails for our AI and increase his success. Now Steve is using AI to directly iterate with Airpeggio. Wouldn't it be better if we were already supervising AI driven development to enhance the success of team members who are trying to use AI without the experience we have?
