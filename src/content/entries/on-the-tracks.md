I stand on the tracks, admiring the strength of the steel. For years, heavy trains have rolled and ground along these rails, which stand still today. Aside from a mark or two they're none the less for wear. I can feel the heat radiating from the searing metal glistening in the sun.

The tracks spill quietly down the horizon until the disappear out of sight. As far as I can tell, there's no train coming. But I can still imagine one. Maybe it's far off. I have plenty of time to get off the tracks and save a great ordeal of pain.

> **Question:** Do I stand here or do I move out of the way?

I watch the train barrelling towards me. My feet pointed straight toward it in defiance. The driver still doesn't see me. Or does he? It's my daydream, so let's make him obvlivious. It's a regular job for him, watching the dials and guages as his giant horse churns across miles of track day after day.

Standing here I'm sure he'll see me eventually. What a nightmare that must be for him. I wonder if he would ever recover. What if I spread myself on the ground tucked narrowly between the girders. If I lay perfectly still, would he kill me without noticing? Would he be able to go home to his family without feeling guilty? Or would I derail the entire machine, giving him nightmares for days to come?

I still stand there as the unstoppable force of the train approaches. Hissing and churning, the might of the engine causes the perfectly sturdy rails to tremble slightly. I feel the pulsing in the soles of my feet. I'm going to have to decide soon. Tiny pebbles begin dancing between the railroad ties, as if excited for the show.

Decision made. I lie on my back, staring up at the sky. I feel the warmth of the wood under my neck. My cheeks red from the heat emanating from the metal. Now the pebbles begin splashing against my face, my skull vibrating from the heavy beams under my neck.

Having made the decision is relaxing, but I feel a heavy sadness. The finality brings a calm warmth throughout my body. My biggest regret is that I won't be around to see the finality dripping through the steel and into the ground. If only I could watch to the end.
