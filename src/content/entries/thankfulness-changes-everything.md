Being thankful can change your world from the inside out. I feel like most people would agree that gratitude is helpful, but some people think gratitude is the result of a life well lived. What if I told you that a life well lived is a result of the gratitude in your life? When you find things to be thankful for, it makes your mind naturally start looking forward to things. When other people see your gratitude, it makes them look forward to being with you or even helping you.

Whether you're a seasoned "gratitude veteran" or you're a novice to the world of thankfulness, there are steps you can practice to up your game. First, start out small.

## Start By Making a Habit

You may be the most thankful person you know; you might be a person who is grateful when there's something to be grateful <u>**for**</u>; or you may be a person who feels like there's nothing to be thankful for. I learned the lesson about gratitude when I felt like I had nothing to look forward to.

It started with a simple practice, which I loathed and felt would never make a difference. Every day before you get out of bed, think of ten things to be thankful for. My very first day, I lay in bed, searching desperately for something to be thankful for:

- I didn't want to thank God that I was alive, because I wasn't sure I wanted to be.
- I didn't want to be thankful for waking up, because I'd rather be asleep.
- I didn't want to thank God that I had a job, because I hated my job.

After groping around in the darkness of my mind, I found a small nugget of gratitude. It was hot outside, but inside we had air conditioning. I'd lived a summer without AC, and it was miserable. So I was thankful for the air conditioning. Come to think of it, I was also grateful that I had a bed to sleep in. As the minutes dragged on for what seemed like hours, I eventually came up with ten things and was released from my obligation.

I continued to practice this habit for an entire year. Some days were easier than others, but after a while, it became second nature to find things to be grateful for. Even when those things were small or when I didn't like the situation I was in. My worldview started to transform from a mundane experience of loathing into a scavenger hunt of tiny pearls of happiness. I even started to feel happy -- even when there wasn't much to be happy about.

## Thank In and Out

Now that you've mastered the practice of digging up something to be thankful for, try saying it out loud. A mentor of mine had a rule.

> Always lead with gratitude. Always leave with gratitude.

If someone invites you over to their house, let the very first thing you say be "Thank you for inviting me." And when you leave, thank them for their hospitality. This practice can flow into many other areas of your life.

At a restaurant, thank the waitstaff as soon as they come to your table. It's super weird at first, but it WORKS. I've never had someone say that was weird, and more often than not I've seen it turn the entire dining experience around. And when I leave, I always thank the server for a great evening. Sometimes it's hard to find them as they've moved on, but when I find them, a quick "thanks again!" makes a huge difference.

One day our server looked like he'd rather be anywhere else. I don't know what he was thinking, but the restaurant was busy, and I was going off the look on his face. I almost asked him about his horrible, terrible, no good, very bad day. Instead I smiled and said, "Thank you for being here today!" He stopped in his tracks and stared at me for a moment. I held my breath, worried I'd somehow messed up. He visibly relaxed and smiled and told us we were the nicest people he'd met all night. Before we left, he said, "I hope y'all come back -- I really enjoyed your company."

At work, when you get called into a meeting, thank them for the opportunity to listen in. If you're leading the meeting, thank everyone for their time. Thank your wife for her cooking. Thank your gardener for his attention to detail. Be so thankful that people just EXPECT that when you call them they're going to get thanked for SOMETHING.

## Graduate With Gratitude for Trouble

If you've graduated from being thankful all the time, there's still more to practice. This lesson might seem like the most contradictory of all. Thank God for troubles in your life.

It might help to turn it into a game. We've been playing on easy levels such as being thankful for ten blessings every day. Now we're on the hard level. You've been fired, you don't know how you're going to pay the bills, and now you have a flat tire. WHAT ON EARTH can you be thankful for?

That's the game! What CAN you be thankful for? That you had a car to have a flat IN? That you have enough gas to run the AC and keep the groceries cool? That you got to meet a stranger? If you've already mastered the other skills, you might even find a cool surprise. You might find that your contagious gratitude makes them happy to have helped you. I've met so many people I'm still friends with today as a result of flats, blowouts, or empty gas tanks. Some even invited me over for dinner.

Sometimes it's better not to try to see AROUND the problem, but rather to see INTO it. Even pain has a purpose in our lives.

<u>**It helps keep us safe**</u>

Pain and sadness were designed to help us be better next time and avoid the same mistake. That in itself is something you can be grateful for. I teach my son to thank himself for making mistakes, because it gives him an opportunity to grow.

<u>**It teaches patience**</u>

Pain increases your tolerance and patience. I remember my son bumping his arm on the wall and writhing in pain. My wife told him he was making a big deal out of nothing, but in that moment I had a glimpse of life through his eyes: he was two years old and this was the worst pain of his entire life. Whatever comes of your pain, tomorrow you will be better equipped to deal with the pain of the past.

<u>**It makes room for happiness**</u>

I used to enjoy one Oreo. Then one day I had two, and before long I was eating four at a time. Eventually I could eat a whole bag and barely notice a single cookie. I took a break from Oreos, and when I finally had one again, one was plenty. I actually tasted it.

Happiness can sneak past me the same way. When things are good for long enough, I get used to them. After a sad stretch, a good day can feel good again in a way I'd forgotten.

*(And now I want an Oreo.)*

## Bonus Level

There are a few extra challenges I give myself. These aren't essential, but rather more like a game within a game that I play myself.

Don't be grateful for things you don't have yet. That includes things you expect to happen tomorrow. There's enough to be thankful for today, and it doesn't risk setting yourself up for the future.

Don't be grateful things aren't worse: *at least I didn't have TWO flats*. That draws your focus back to the problem in a way. You can still be grateful for the benefits you have amid a problem, but gratitude that things aren't worse tends to be a little hollow.
